Get involved

The program needs three things: organisations willing to host a class, people willing to lead one, and clinicians willing to govern it. Each is set out below, with what it actually asks of you.

Host a program Become a leader Support as a clinician

Host a program

For neighbourhood centres, COTA branches, Men's Sheds, service clubs, Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisations, councils and church halls — any organisation with a room and a membership.

What you provide

  • A room, once a week, for about ninety minutes including setting up and tea. It needs a flat floor, sturdy chairs and a toilet. It does not need to be a gym.
  • One or two members willing to train as leaders. If you do not have anyone yet, we can help you recruit.
  • Your existing public liability and volunteer insurance, extended to cover the class as an activity of your organisation.
  • An administrative contact who keeps the participant list and passes incident forms on. Roughly an hour a month.

What you receive

  • The full program: exercise content, screening forms, participant records, incident reporting and site agreement, ready to use.
  • Training and accreditation for your leaders, at no cost to them or to you.
  • A named clinician for your site, and scheduled review visits.
  • Promotional material for your local area, and help with the first enrolments.
  • A weekly activity that brings people through your doors for years, not weeks.

What we ask before you commit

Only host if your organisation expects to still be running this in three years. A class that starts and stops does more harm than one that never starts, because the people who came have lost something. If you are not sure, talk to us first and we will work through it with you.

Become a leader

Leaders are ordinary people from the community. Most are older people themselves. You do not need a health qualification, a fitness background or any experience teaching. Many leaders start as participants.

What the training involves

  • Two days of training, run locally, delivered by a physiotherapist.
  • You learn the exercise sequence, how to adapt it for different people, how to progress someone safely, how to spot a person who should sit out that day, and exactly when to stop and refer.
  • You then co-lead a few classes alongside an experienced leader before running one yourself.
  • A half-day refresher each year.
  • Training is free. Travel costs to training are covered.

What the commitment is

  • About ninety minutes a week, including setting up and tea afterwards.
  • Leading in pairs is normal and encouraged, so you are not stuck if you are away or unwell.
  • We ask for a twelve-month commitment to begin with. Most leaders stay considerably longer.

Who it suits

People who are reliable, warm with a group, and comfortable saying “that is not for me to advise on, let's ask the physio”. That last one matters most. Leaders run a set program and refer when something changes. They do not assess, diagnose or treat, and the training is clear about where the line is.

Just want to join a class?

You are equally welcome, and there is no obligation to lead. Email us and we will tell you what is running near you, or let you know when a class starts in your area.

Support the program as a clinician

For physiotherapists, exercise physiologists and GPs. Your time is the binding constraint on this program, so here is the ask up front, before anything else.

The time commitment

Establishment year
Roughly 10–15 hours in total, spread across the year.
Each year after that
Roughly 4–8 hours a year.

These are the program author's estimates, based on the model described in the four points of clinical input. They are not drawn from an evaluated Australian program, and we will publish actual figures once sites have been running for a year.

What those hours are spent on

  • Leader training — the largest single block, and mostly in the first year.
  • Review visits — sitting in on a class, typically quarterly once the site is settled, to check progression and answer the leaders' questions.
  • Being the named contact — occasional calls or emails from leaders about a specific participant. These are short.
  • Reviewing incident reports — a small number of one-page forms.

What you are not being asked to do

  • Run the weekly class. That is the whole point of the model.
  • Take on the participants as patients, unless you choose to when someone is referred to you.
  • Carry the host organisation's insurance or administrative obligations.

Practical matters

You will receive a written role description setting out the scope of the governance role, which you can give to your professional indemnity insurer before you commit. Governance activity is intended to sit within your normal scope of practice, but confirm the position with your own insurer — we would rather you check than assume.

Whether the role is paid, sessionally funded or voluntary depends on how the site is funded. We will tell you which applies at your site before you agree to anything.
