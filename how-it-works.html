How it works

Volunteers deliver the class. Clinicians govern it. No clinician is needed in the room each week.

A class, in short

Where
A local hall or neighbourhood centre. Sturdy chairs, flat floor. No gym.
When
One hour, same time every week.
How long for
Indefinitely. There is no final session.
Who leads
One or two trained volunteers, usually older people themselves.
What happens
Warm-up, then set balance and leg-strength exercises, standing with a chair to hold. Most can be done seated.
And then
Tea. Turning up for years is a social act before it is a clinical one.

Where the clinical time goes

Four points, and no others.

  1. Content design

    A physiotherapist sets the exercises, the progressions and the limits. Done once, then reviewed. Not repeated per site.

  2. Leader training

    Volunteers are trained and accredited before they lead. Largely a first-year cost.

  3. Progression review

    A clinician sits in on a class, typically quarterly, to check progression is appropriate.

  4. Escalation and referral

    Leaders have a named clinician and a written threshold for calling them.

Is it safe? Who is responsible?

Before joining
Everyone completes a short health screen. Anyone outside the program's scope is referred to their GP first, not turned away.
What leaders never do
Assess, diagnose, treat, advise on medication, or invent exercises. They run a set program and refer when something changes.
Who carries what
The host organisation carries venue, records and insurance. The clinical tier carries the program content and leader accreditation. Each is written into the site agreement.
Insurance
The host's existing public liability and volunteer cover extends to the class as one of its activities. Clinicians receive a written role description for their indemnity insurer.
Incidents
Every fall or near miss goes on a one-page form to the clinical tier, reviewed across sites so patterns show up early.

The honest comparison

Exercise carries some risk. The comparison is not against a perfectly safe alternative. It is against what most regional towns have now, which is no program and a population getting less steady each year.

Does it work?

Steady As You Go, New Zealand
Peer-led, runs continuously. People stay 4.3 years on average, some ten.
Stepping On, NSW
Cut falls by 31% in trial. Ran at 1,077 sites with 10,096 participants from 2009 to 2014.
A Matter of Balance, United States
A clinician-led program deliberately redesigned for volunteer lay leaders. The redesign is what let it spread.
FallFitness, Sweden
Studied lay-led against professionally led delivery directly. The clearest precedent for this model.

What the evidence does not show

No trial has directly compared volunteer-led with clinician-led delivery on falls outcomes. Clinician-led delivery may well produce better short-term results. But in most regional communities the real choice is a volunteer-led class that runs for years, or nothing.
