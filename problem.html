The challenge

We know how to prevent many falls. In most regional communities, the prevention never arrives.

The scale of falls

  • 41,000

    NSW hospital admissions after a fall, each year.

    Clinical Excellence Commission, 2021
  • 1,200+

    Deaths from falls in NSW, each year.

    Clinical Excellence Commission, 2021
  • A$4.3–5bn

    Annual cost of falls to the Australian health system.

    AIHW

By 2051, NSW fall hospitalisations are projected to run about three times higher on population ageing alone, and more than ten times higher if recent trends continue. Transfers into aged care after a fall are projected to rise 3.2 times.

No realistic increase in hospital capacity absorbs that.

Why prevention doesn't reach us

Falls prevention is not an unsolved problem. NSW has run Stepping On at real scale. The constraint is not knowledge. It is delivery.

Most proven programs need a physiotherapist in the room every week, for a fixed block of sessions. Two things follow.

  • They run where clinicians are plentiful, which is largely where the population is dense.
  • They end. Balance declines again once training stops, so a ten-week block hands the person back to the same risk.

A design problem, not a performance problem

None of this is a criticism of rural clinicians. They carry heavier caseloads with less backup and longer distances than their city colleagues. They are not failing to run these programs. They are fully occupied.

If a model needs scarce clinical hours every week, indefinitely, it will not reach most regional communities. The model is what has to change.

So a program that survives out here must

  • Run without a clinician in the room each week.
  • Be ongoing, not a block.
  • Stay clinically governed, because safety is a clinical question.
  • Be cheap enough for a town of 900 people.
